Unstable with a pulse means any one of: hypotension, acutely altered mental status, signs of shock, ischemic chest discomfort, or acute heart failure. Unstable tachycardia gets synchronized cardioversion. Symptomatic bradycardia gets atropine, then pacing.

Shock or no shock

SituationWhat the ED nurse prepares
V-fib, pulseless V-tachCPR and defibrillation (unsynchronized). Epinephrine once initial shocks have failed.
PEA, asystoleCPR and epinephrine 1 mg as soon as possible, then every 3 to 5 minutes. No shock.
Unstable tachycardia with a pulseSynchronized cardioversion. Sedate if feasible.
Symptomatic bradycardia, heart blockAtropine 1 mg IV. If it fails, or the block is high grade: pacing, or dopamine or epinephrine infusion.
Stable tachycardiaSVT: vagal maneuver, then adenosine. A-fib and flutter: rate control. V-tach: antiarrhythmic infusion with expert help.
Normal sinus, sinus tach, sinus bradyTreat the cause and the patient, not the number.

Doses and numbers

Atropine
1 mg IV, repeat every 3 to 5 min, maximum 3 mg
Adenosine
6 mg rapid IV push with flush, then 12 mg if needed
Epinephrine (arrest)
1 mg IV (IO if no IV) every 3 to 5 min
Amiodarone (arrest, VF or pVT)
300 mg, then 150 mg
Amiodarone (stable VT)
150 mg IV over 10 min
Lidocaine (arrest)
1 to 1.5 mg/kg, then 0.5 to 0.75 mg/kg
Dopamine infusion
5 to 20 mcg/kg/min
Epinephrine infusion (bradycardia)
2 to 10 mcg/min
Compressions
100 to 120 per min, at least 5 cm (2 in) deep, full recoil, short pauses
